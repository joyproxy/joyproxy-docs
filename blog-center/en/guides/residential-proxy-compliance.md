# Residential Proxy Compliance: Acceptable Use and Risk Boundaries

Residential proxy networks route real consumer ISP allocations. That benefits legitimate geo testing and public data collection—but it also means providers and regulators scrutinize abuse aggressively. JoyProxy’s [Terms & Conditions](https://www.joyproxy.com/terms.html) define acceptable use; this article explains the practical boundaries teams ask about.

## Generally legitimate patterns

  * Scraping or accessing **public** pages you have rights to access, at sustainable rates.
  * Testing your own applications from multiple regions.
  * Ad placement verification contracted by advertisers.
  * Market research on publicly listed prices (where not prohibited by site terms).



## Prohibited uses (non-exhaustive)

JoyProxy explicitly bans illegal activity, malware distribution, credential stuffing, harassment, fraud, and unauthorized intrusion. Residential IPs must not be used to circumvent security of systems you do not own or lack permission to test.

## Your responsibilities as the customer

  * Read target site terms and applicable law in your jurisdiction—proxy usage does not transfer liability.
  * Protect API tokens, proxy passwords, and AI Access Tokens.
  * Honor robots.txt and rate limits where they reflect contractual limits you agreed to.
  * Do not resell or share JoyProxy endpoints outside your organization’s entitlement.



## Data handling

JoyProxy’s [Privacy Policy](https://www.joyproxy.com/privacy.html) states that proxy transit content is not logged for browsing surveillance; generator request metadata and traffic accounting are kept for billing and abuse prevention. Treat data you collect through proxies under your own compliance program (GDPR, sector rules, etc.).

**Not legal advice**

This article summarizes product policy, not legal counsel. For regulated industries (finance, health), involve your compliance team before production deployment.
